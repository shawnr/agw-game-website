    Sources & Customers

    Every building you'll route mana to or from. Numbers here are each building's standard values. Operations sometimes adjust a particular building's needs or output, so the in-game tooltip always has the final word.

    Reading the numbers

    • Output is mana produced per day. It's split evenly across the building's outgoing conduits, and a source can have at most two.
    • Needs is mana required per day, for each type.
    • Pays is daily revenue when fully supplied and Content: total needs × 2 GC. See Revenue.
    • Sensitivity is how fast the building gets frustrated when it's short. Each day it gains (shortfall × sensitivity) frustration. Higher means touchier. See Customer satisfaction.

    Sources

    Sources produce one type of mana, 30 per day, forever, for free. They never get frustrated. Since a basic conduit carries 15, it takes two conduits (or one upgraded conduit) to use a source's full output.

    Gas Pocket

    Trapped underground gas. Many maps don't have one, so plan on a hybrid or Transformer.

    Mixed sources

    Three campaign operations (The Filtering Problem, The Intersection and Explosive Neighbors) have sources that produce two types at once: 15 of each per day. Operations give them local names (a Mud Spring might be called a bog, for instance), but hovering one always shows what it produces. Mixed sources never appear in Freelance or Form 27-B jobs.

    You have two ways to use one:

    • Raw. A conduit drawn straight from a mixed source is a raw mixed line carrying both types. It must run directly to a customer that needs both types, it can't feed any component, and its two types react with each other along its whole length.
    • Filtered. Place a Filter on a hex next to the source to send out just one type, on an insulated line you can route anywhere. Shift+Click the Filter to choose which type.

    Mixed sources put out two energy types on one conduit. A raw line can only feed a consumer that needs both. Place a Filter on the hex next to the source to send out just one type.

    Hybrids

    Hybrids are customers that also produce. They need mana like any customer (and pay for it, and get frustrated without it), but while all of their needs are met they also put out a different type. If any need falls short, production stops completely, and everything downstream goes dark with it.

    Tip

    Hybrid output follows the same rules as any source: it's split evenly across the hybrid's outgoing conduits, and dissipation still applies on the way out.
